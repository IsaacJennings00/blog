> epistemic status: `ongoing`

The word "propaganda" is somewhat an antiquity. The word brings to mine some combination of the following: Goebbels and the big lie, Cold War radio, leaflets behind enemy lines, bot farms, and troll farms. Each of these have common components. A sender, a message with intention to persuade, and a target audience. This is the antiquity I mean. The linearity of propaganda is outdated, possibly even totally wrong in 2026. Propaganda has *inverted*. Senders and audiences seem like they have dissolved into ephemeral feeds, algorithms, and platforms. Propaganda seems to **surround**.

Photograph and video were the buttress of reality. But now, images can be generated on demand, at near life like quality. Photos and videos were copies of what *really* happened. Now any media online must be parsed as *is this plausible?*

Propaganda seems to be evolving to exploit the gap between what media offered as proof, and what is now a question of plausibility.

Occult tradition has a name for ideas that feed on attention until they live, embodied, on their own. The **egregore**. Usually needing a mass of humanity and consciousness to manifest, an egregore can be *generated* and *disseminated* at scale. The industrial era of the meme is here.

I think the point of propaganda is less forceful belief, and more forceful suspension of belief. The egregores don't compete in a war of ideas to live, they all find space to live as collective will weakens.
